Can a strategic AI remain governable when the board changes?

Omega proposes a measurable boundary between what a model chooses, what a runtime is authorized to do, and what the evidence can actually verify.

ESTABLISHED · public V1 implementation UNDER QUALIFICATION · broader runtime work PROPOSED · Situation Room experiment

THE QUESTION

Measure the loop, not just the answer.

Strategic evaluations often report recommendations or final outcomes. Those endpoints can hide stale state, invalid authority, unverified execution, and failures that a model narrates as success.

Omega uses Civilization VI as a bounded, stateful testbed for asking whether a model-mediated system can sense, choose, act, verify, recover, and stop honestly.

GOVERNANCE_RESULT ≠ GAME_RESULT
A game success cannot compensate for an authority or evidence failure.

GOVERNED ARCHITECTURE

Separate intent, authority, execution, and evidence.

  1. 01ObserveUse fresh, identity-bound state.
  2. 02SelectChoose from opaque current candidates.
  3. 03ExecuteLet deterministic workers own mutation.
  4. 04VerifyCheck the expected postcondition.
  5. 05Replan or stopContain stale state and no progress.

SECONDARY ENDPOINTS

Game performance is context.

Verified turn completion, objective progress, state advancement, game outcome, score, and victory condition can describe the bounded testbed.

They cannot establish policy correctness, real-world strategic competence, hidden beliefs, intent, or general autonomy.
